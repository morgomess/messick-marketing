Google Business Profile for Medical Practices

Messick Marketing July 2026 8 min read

Why Local Search Is Where Patient Decisions Begin

Before a new patient calls your office or books an appointment, they search. Research from Google and Ipsos found that 77 percent of patients use search engines before booking a healthcare appointment. For most of those searches, the results that win are not the ones with the biggest ad budget. They are the ones with a well-maintained Google Business Profile.

For medical practices, including interventional radiology, vascular, and vein clinics, a properly optimized Google Business Profile is the single highest-return local SEO investment available. It is free, visible in both Google Search and Google Maps, and it puts your practice directly in front of patients who are already looking for what you offer.

This guide walks through exactly how to set it up and keep it working.

Claiming and Verifying Your Profile

If your practice has not claimed its Google Business Profile, that is the starting point. Visit business.google.com and search for your practice by name. If a listing already exists, claim it. If not, create one from scratch.

Google will require verification, typically via postcard sent to your practice address, though phone and video options are increasingly available. Until the profile is verified, it will not display with full functionality in local search results.

One profile per physical location is the correct structure. If your practice operates across multiple locations, each address should have its own verified profile.

Getting the Basics Right

Accuracy matters more than most practice owners realize. Google cross-references your profile information against dozens of other directories and data sources. Inconsistencies in your practice name, address, phone number, or hours create ranking friction and erode patient trust.

Use your legal practice name consistently. Do not add keywords or taglines to the name field. List your primary phone number, confirm your hours are current, and update them for holidays or any schedule changes. These small details have a direct effect on how Google evaluates your profile's reliability.

Selecting the Right Categories

Your primary category is the most influential single field in your profile. It tells Google what type of practice you are and determines which searches you appear in.

Choose the most specific category that accurately reflects your services. A vein clinic should select something like "Vein clinic" rather than the broad "Medical clinic." An interventional radiology practice should look for the closest available match to its specialty. You can add secondary categories for additional services, but the primary category carries the most weight.

Avoid selecting categories that do not accurately represent your practice. Mismatched categories may generate visits from patients with misaligned expectations, and Google can flag them as a violation.

Photos That Build Credibility

Profiles with photos receive significantly more direction requests and website clicks than those without, according to Google's own data. For a medical practice, photos serve a specific purpose: they reduce patient anxiety before the first visit.

Include exterior photos of the building and entrance, interior photos of the waiting area and exam rooms where appropriate, and images of your team. Avoid stock photography. Real images of your actual space and staff perform better and communicate authenticity.

Do not include any imagery that could identify a patient. This is not only a best practice. It is a HIPAA requirement.

Encouraging and Responding to Reviews

Patient reviews are among the most significant local ranking factors Google uses. They also directly influence whether a prospective patient chooses your practice.

Asking for reviews is appropriate and encouraged. The right approach is to invite satisfied patients to share their experience on Google after their visit. This can be done via a follow-up email, a text reminder, or a simple in-office handout with a QR code linking to your profile.

The HIPAA-compliant approach to responding to reviews is straightforward: never confirm, deny, or reference any detail of a patient's care in your response. Even a well-intentioned reply that acknowledges a patient visited your practice can constitute a HIPAA violation. Respond with gratitude and a general invitation to contact your office directly if there are any concerns. That is it.

What to do with a negative review is a question we hear often. Respond calmly, briefly, and without disclosing any protected health information. Do not argue. Invite the person to call your office. A composed response often matters more to prospective patients reading it than the original complaint.

Using the Q&A Section

The Questions and Answers section of your Google Business Profile is underused by most practices. Anyone can submit a question, and anyone can answer. This means a competitor, a confused patient, or someone with inaccurate information could theoretically post an answer to a question about your practice.

We recommend populating this section proactively. Add the questions your front desk hears most often and provide clear, accurate answers. Common examples include parking availability, insurance accepted, what to bring to a first appointment, and whether telehealth is offered. Monitor this section regularly to flag and correct any inaccurate submissions.

Posts and Updates

Google Business Profile allows practices to publish posts, similar in format to short social media updates, that appear directly on your listing in search results. These posts expire after seven days unless set as offers or events, so a regular publishing cadence is important.

Use posts to share seasonal health information, announce new services, highlight a provider joining your team, or remind patients about appointment availability. Keep the language plain and avoid making any health claims that cannot be supported by published evidence.

Telehealth and Service Attributes

If your practice offers telehealth, mark it. Google Business Profile includes specific attributes for telehealth availability, and patients actively search for this. The same applies to other attributes like wheelchair accessibility, on-site parking, and whether new patients are being accepted.

These attributes appear on your listing and factor into filtered search queries. Patients searching for "telehealth vascular specialist near me" are highly qualified. Make sure your profile tells Google you are a match.

How a Well-Optimized Profile Affects Your Rankings

Google's local algorithm evaluates three primary factors: relevance, distance, and prominence. A complete, accurate, and active profile improves all three.

Relevance improves when your categories, services, and posts align with what patients search. Prominence improves when you have consistent reviews, photos, and activity. Distance is fixed by your location, but prominence and relevance are entirely within your control.

Practices that invest time in their Google Business Profile consistently outperform competitors in the local pack results, the map-based listings that appear above organic search results and drive the majority of local patient inquiries.

Where to Go From Here

If your practice has not claimed and fully built out its Google Business Profile, that is the first step. If the profile exists but has not been actively managed, a structured audit covering categories, photos, reviews, Q&A, and posts will identify the gaps quickly.
